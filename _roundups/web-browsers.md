The browser is the single most-used piece of software on most people's computers, which makes its licensing, privacy defaults, and extension ecosystem matter more than almost any other application choice. Open source browsers let you (or a community of auditors) actually verify what the browser does with your data, rather than trusting a vendor's privacy policy.

Firefox remains the most widely used independent, open source browser engine outside the Chromium family. LibreWolf is a hardened, privacy-focused Firefox fork with tracking protection enabled by default. Tor Browser routes traffic through the Tor network for strong anonymity. Brave is Chromium-based with open source code and built-in ad/tracker blocking.
