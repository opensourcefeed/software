Chat and messaging apps handle some of the most sensitive traffic on your devices, from work discussions to personal conversations, which makes it worth knowing exactly how the software works and where your messages actually go. Open source communication tools let you self-host your own server, audit the encryption, or simply avoid a platform that mines conversations for advertising data.

Signal and Session focus on private, encrypted personal messaging. Element is a Matrix client built for decentralized, federated chat. Mattermost, Rocket.Chat, and Zulip are self-hostable team chat platforms aimed at replacing tools like Slack or Microsoft Teams for organizations that want to keep their data in-house.
