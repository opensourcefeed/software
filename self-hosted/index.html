What self-hosting involves

Self-hosting means running a service yourself instead of renting it from a cloud provider. The appeal is control: your files, messages, and media stay on your own machine, there are no per-user subscriptions, and no company can change the terms or shut the service down. Most tools here are free and open source; a few offer paid editions with extra features, which their pages note.

The practical requirements are modest but real:

  • A machine that stays on: a mini PC, an old laptop, a NAS that runs containers, or a small virtual private server. Media transcoding and local AI models need more power.
  • An install method: most projects ship official Docker images or Compose files, which make installing and updating straightforward.
  • Secure access: keep services on your home network or reach them through a VPN; if you expose them to the internet, use HTTPS and strong authentication.
  • Backups and updates: you're now the administrator, so schedule backups and apply security updates.
