Switching from Canva to GIMP or Inkscape trades a template-driven, cloud-hosted design tool for professional, freeform editors you run and store data on yourself. GIMP covers photo editing and raster composition (social graphics, image touch-ups), while Inkscape handles vector work (logos, icons, print layouts) built around the open SVG standard. Neither replicates Canva's core appeal — a large library of ready-made templates and a drag-and-drop editor aimed at non-designers — so both require more manual design work and a steeper learning curve to reach a finished result. Someone who mainly resizes and tweaks existing templates will feel that gap immediately; someone comfortable building a design from a blank canvas gets full local control and no dependency on Canva's cloud account in return.
