Switching from Discord to an open source alternative gives a community control over its own infrastructure instead of depending entirely on a single company's servers staying up and its terms of service staying the same. Element, built on the federated Matrix protocol, offers rooms and spaces comparable to Discord's servers and channels, along with end-to-end encrypted voice and text and the option to self-host. It doesn't yet match Discord's bot ecosystem or gaming-focused polish, but it removes the vendor lock-in that comes with a fully proprietary chat platform. Zulip is also included here, though honestly it's a less natural Discord substitute than a Slack one: its topic-threaded model is built for organized, asynchronous team and project discussion rather than the fast, casual, voice-first hangout feel that defines Discord communities, and it doesn't have Discord's voice channels or bot/gaming ecosystem. Communities that specifically want Discord's real-time voice-and-text social feel will find Element the closer match; those whose "Discord" is really a topic-organized project or study group with heavy async discussion may prefer Zulip's threading despite the different vibe.
