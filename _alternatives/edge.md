Switching from Microsoft Edge to an open source alternative means leaving behind its Microsoft account sync and Copilot integration for a browser you can inspect and configure independently. Firefox offers a fully separate engine (Gecko rather than Chromium/Blink) and strong tracking protection with no account required. Brave stays on the same Chromium/Blink engine Edge uses, so site and extension compatibility carries over directly, while stripping out Microsoft's telemetry and account services in favor of built-in ad and tracker blocking. LibreWolf takes Firefox's own privacy settings and hardens them further by default. Tor Browser, a Firefox ESR fork maintained by The Tor Project, serves a different purpose entirely — routing traffic through Tor's relay network for anonymity and censorship circumvention rather than for faster, everyday private browsing, at the cost of speed and occasional site blocking. All are free of the bundled AI assistant and default-browser prompts that come with Edge on Windows.
