Switching from Unity to an open source alternative removes any risk of future licensing changes, royalties, or per-install fees, a concern that became prominent after Unity's 2023 pricing controversy. Open source game engines are distributed under permissive licenses with no revenue thresholds or recurring costs, and their source code can be inspected or modified directly. For most 2D and indie 3D game development, this alternative covers Unity's core workflow — scene editing, scripting, and cross-platform export — without licensing uncertainty.
