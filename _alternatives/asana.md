Switching from Asana to an open source alternative means giving up its hosted convenience for self-managed infrastructure and, in exchange, no per-seat subscription cost and full control over your project data. OpenProject is the closer match for teams that rely on Asana's timeline views, custom fields, and cross-project reporting — it covers Gantt charts, work packages, and agile boards in a self-hosted package. Taiga sits between the other two: it's built specifically around Scrum and Kanban workflows, with a cleaner, more opinionated interface than OpenProject's broader feature set, making it a good fit for agile software teams that want backlogs, sprints, and burndown charts without OpenProject's configuration overhead. Wekan is the lightest option if what you actually use is just kanban boards and task lists, trading timeline and portfolio features for a simpler, Trello-like interface. None matches Asana's polish or third-party integration catalog exactly, but all three eliminate the recurring per-user cost as your team grows.
