Switching from Adobe Lightroom to an open source alternative eliminates the Creative Cloud subscription while keeping the core workflow: non-destructive RAW editing. darktable is the closer match to Lightroom's own model, pairing a lighttable-and-darkroom interface with library-wide cataloging — it stores every adjustment as a reversible history stack, just like Lightroom, and covers exposure, color, noise reduction, and lens correction through combinable processing modules, while also running natively on Linux where Lightroom has no client at all. RawTherapee takes a different approach: rather than an imported catalog, it works directly on folders using sidecar files, and processes every image through a high-precision 32-bit floating-point pipeline with a choice of demosaicing algorithms, wavelet-based local adjustments, and Retinex tone mapping — giving it deeper technical control over RAW conversion at the cost of darktable's catalog-style organization. Both trade Lightroom's streamlined panels and large third-party preset ecosystem for a steeper, more technical interface.
