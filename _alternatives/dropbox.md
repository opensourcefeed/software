Switching from Dropbox to an open source alternative removes recurring subscription costs and lets you keep files on infrastructure you control instead of a third-party server. Open source file sync tools range from self-hosted platforms with a full web interface and app ecosystem to lightweight peer-to-peer sync with no central server at all, so you can choose the model that fits your privacy and hosting needs. Nextcloud is the broadest option, pairing file sync with calendars, video calls, and a wide app ecosystem in one groupware suite. Syncthing skips servers entirely, syncing files directly between your own devices over encrypted peer-to-peer connections. Seafile sits between the two, focused specifically on fast sync and sharing with library-based version history — its Community Edition is open source, though some enterprise features are reserved for a separate paid Professional Edition. For most personal and team file-sharing workflows, these alternatives cover Dropbox's core sync and share capabilities at no licensing cost.
