DaVinci Resolve's free edition is generous, but the application itself is closed source, and its most advanced color, audio, and VFX tools remain locked behind a paid Studio upgrade. Kdenlive and Shotcut are both fully open source, mature non-linear editors built on the MLT Framework, covering multi-track editing, color correction, and a wide range of effects and filters without any paid tier at all. OpenShot trades some of that depth for a simpler, more approachable interface, and Olive offers a node-based compositing workflow but is still earlier in development, with a smaller feature set than the others. None of the four matches Resolve's dedicated color-grading depth or its integrated Fairlight audio and Fusion compositing tools, but for straightforward timeline-based editing, color correction, and export, they cover the same core workflow as fully auditable, community-maintained alternatives.
