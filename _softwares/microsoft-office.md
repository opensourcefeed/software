## Overview

Microsoft Office was announced by Bill Gates on August 1, 1988, at COMDEX and officially released on October 1, 1990, bundling Word, Excel, and PowerPoint into a single suite for the first time. Those three applications remain the core of the product line today, later joined by Outlook, Access, and OneNote. Office 97 introduced the toolbar-based "Office Assistant" era and laid groundwork for later interface changes, Office 2007 introduced the ribbon interface and the Office Open XML file formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx), and Office 2013 marked Microsoft's pivot toward cloud-connected features and subscription delivery.

Microsoft launched Office 365, a subscription-based version of the suite delivered with regular updates and cloud services, in 2011. In 2020, Microsoft rebranded the consumer and business subscription lines to Microsoft 365, reflecting the addition of services such as OneDrive cloud storage, Microsoft Teams, and Defender security tools alongside the core apps. Microsoft continues to sell perpetual-license versions in parallel, with Office 2021 and Office 2024 as the most recent one-time-purchase releases, though these lack the continuous feature updates and cloud integrations included with Microsoft 365 subscriptions. The applications are available on Windows and macOS as desktop installs, on iOS and Android as mobile apps, and through Office on the web for browser-based editing, which also provides access on Linux and Chrome OS.

## Key Features

- **Word** — Word processing with styles, templates, track changes, and co-authoring
- **Excel** — Spreadsheets with formulas, pivot tables, charts, and Power Query data import
- **PowerPoint** — Slide-based presentation creation with themes, transitions, and speaker notes
- **Outlook** — Email, calendar, and contact management integrated with Exchange
- **OneDrive integration** — Cloud storage and file synchronization across devices
- **Real-time co-authoring** — Multiple users editing the same document simultaneously
- **Copilot AI features** — AI-assisted writing, data analysis, and slide generation on eligible subscription tiers

## Use Cases

- **Office workers** — Drafting reports, managing spreadsheets, and preparing presentations
- **Students and educators** — Writing papers, creating study materials, and classroom presentations
- **Small businesses** — Invoicing, budgeting, and correspondence with clients
- **Teams and organizations** — Shared document collaboration via OneDrive and SharePoint

## Who It's For

Microsoft Office is aimed at individuals, students, and organizations that need document creation, spreadsheet, and presentation tools with broad file-format compatibility. It is distributed through Microsoft 365 subscription plans (Personal, Family, Business, and Enterprise tiers) or as a one-time purchase (Office Home & Student, Office Home & Business) for users who prefer perpetual licensing over a subscription.
