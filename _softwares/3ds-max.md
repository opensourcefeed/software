## Overview

3ds Max traces back to 3D Studio, released by the Yost Group for Autodesk in 1990 for MS-DOS. It was rewritten for Windows NT and rebranded 3D Studio MAX in 1996, and Autodesk acquired full ownership of the product from Kinetix (its original developer division) in the early 2000s, later shortening the name to 3ds Max. Unlike Maya, which is cross-platform, 3ds Max has always been Windows-only, and this focus has made it particularly popular in game development and architectural visualization studios that standardize on Windows workstations.

3ds Max and Maya are both owned by Autodesk and share significant overlap in capability, but each has retained a distinct user base: 3ds Max is traditionally stronger in polygon modeling workflows favored by game artists and architectural visualization studios, while Maya has been more dominant in film and TV animation pipelines.

## Key Features

- **Polygon and spline modeling** — Modifier stack-based workflow for non-destructive polygon and NURBS modeling
- **Character animation** — Biped and CAT (Character Animation Toolkit) rigging systems
- **Architectural visualization tools** — Built-in tools and asset libraries geared toward arch-viz rendering
- **Arnold renderer** — Integrated physically based renderer, shared with Maya
- **Modifier stack** — Non-destructive, stackable modeling operations that can be reordered or edited after the fact
- **MAXScript and Python scripting** — Automation and custom tool development

## Use Cases

- **Game asset creation** — Modeling and texturing environment and character assets for game engines
- **Architectural visualization** — Photorealistic renders and walkthroughs of building designs
- **Product visualization** — Renders and animations for product design and marketing
- **Motion graphics** — 3D elements for broadcast and commercial motion graphics work

## Who It's For

3ds Max is aimed at game artists, architectural visualization specialists, and product designers working on Windows who need a modeling and rendering pipeline geared toward polygon-heavy asset creation. Its Windows-only availability and subscription pricing make it a studio-oriented tool rather than a casual or cross-platform option.
