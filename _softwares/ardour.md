## Overview

Ardour was started in 1999 by Paul Davis, who also created the JACK Audio Connection Kit, with the goal of building a professional-grade, Linux-native digital audio workstation comparable to Pro Tools. It reached a stable 1.0 release in 2005 and has been developed continuously since, with support for Windows and macOS added in later versions alongside its original Linux focus.

The project is funded through a mix of paid builds and voluntary subscriptions — official pre-built binaries require payment or a subscription, though the source code is fully GPL-licensed and can be compiled for free by anyone willing to build it themselves. Ardour is built around JACK and ALSA on Linux, and supports LV2, VST, and Audio Unit plugins across platforms.

## Key Features

- **Unlimited tracks and busses** — Multitrack recording and mixing with no artificial track-count limits
- **MIDI editing and virtual instruments** — Full MIDI sequencing alongside audio, with plugin instrument support
- **Non-destructive editing** — Full undo history and non-destructive audio region editing
- **Extensive plugin support** — LV2, VST2/VST3, LADSPA, and Audio Unit (macOS) compatibility
- **Automation** — Sample-accurate automation of volume, panning, and plugin parameters
- **Video and MIDI sync** — Timeline sync with external video and MIDI timecode sources

## Use Cases

- **Multitrack music recording** — Studio-style recording and mixing sessions with unlimited tracks
- **Post-production audio** — Dialogue editing and sound mixing synced to video
- **MIDI composition** — Sequencing virtual instruments alongside recorded audio
- **Live sound archiving** — Recording and editing multitrack live performance audio

## Who It's For

Ardour is aimed at musicians, engineers, and studios who need a full-featured DAW comparable to Pro Tools or Logic Pro without a subscription, and who are comfortable with either paying for a pre-built binary or compiling from source. It suits users doing serious multitrack recording and mixing work, rather than quick single-track edits.
