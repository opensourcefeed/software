## Overview

OBS Studio (Open Broadcaster Software) is a free and open source application for screen recording and live streaming, maintained by the OBS Project. It composites multiple video and audio sources — windows, displays, cameras, and browser sources — into scenes, and can record that output to a local file or stream it directly to services like YouTube, Twitch, and any custom RTMP or SRT destination. It is the de facto standard screen-capture and streaming tool across Windows, macOS, and Linux, used by streamers, educators, and anyone recording tutorials or presentations.

## Key Features

- **Scene compositing** — Combine windows, displays, webcams, images, and browser sources into layered scenes with transitions between them
- **Live streaming** — Stream directly to YouTube, Twitch, and other RTMP/SRT/RIST-compatible services, or to multiple destinations at once
- **High-performance recording** — Hardware-accelerated encoding (NVENC, Quick Sync, AMF) for low-overhead capture at high resolutions and frame rates
- **Audio mixer** — Per-source volume control, noise suppression, noise gate, and VST plugin support
- **Studio Mode** — Preview and adjust the next scene before switching it live
- **Plugin and scripting support** — A plugin API and Lua/Python scripting for custom sources, filters, and automation
- **Cross-platform** — Native builds for Windows, macOS, and Linux (including Flatpak)

## Use Cases

- **Live streaming** — Broadcasting gameplay, talks, or other live video to streaming platforms
- **Screen recording** — Capturing tutorials, software walkthroughs, and presentations
- **Video conferencing production** — Using OBS as a virtual camera to send composited scenes into video calls
- **Content creation** — Recording multi-source video for later editing, with separate audio tracks per source

## Who It's For

OBS Studio suits streamers, educators, and anyone who needs to record or broadcast their screen without paying for a subscription or depending on closed-source capture software. Its scene compositing and audio mixing cover far more than basic screen recording, making it a common choice for production setups with multiple cameras and sources, while its simpler presets still work for a single-window recording.
