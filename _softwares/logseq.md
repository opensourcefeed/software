## Overview

Logseq began as an open source project focused on outliner-style note-taking, drawing inspiration from tools like Roam Research and TiddlyWiki but committing to local-first, plain-text storage from the start. Rather than a proprietary database like Notion's, Logseq notes are stored as ordinary Markdown or Org-mode files on the user's own device, which means they remain readable and portable even without the app, and can be synced through any file-syncing tool the user already trusts.

The project is released under the AGPL-3.0 license and is developed openly, with a company (Logseq Inc.) funding core development while keeping the client free to use. Its outliner model — every line is a bullet that can be collapsed, referenced, and linked — combined with automatic backlinks makes it well suited to networked note-taking, journaling, and building a personal knowledge graph, though it lacks the structured relational databases and polished template ecosystem Notion offers.

## Key Features

- **Local-first storage** — Notes saved as plain Markdown/Org files on your own device
- **Outliner structure** — Every note is built from nested, collapsible bullets
- **Bi-directional linking** — Automatic backlinks between pages that reference each other
- **Daily journal** — A running daily notes page as the default entry point
- **Knowledge graph view** — Visualize connections between notes
- **Plugin ecosystem** — Community plugins for extending functionality
- **Optional end-to-end encrypted sync** — Paid sync service, though local storage works without it

## Use Cases

- **Personal knowledge management** — Building a linked, searchable second brain
- **Daily journaling** — Quick daily notes that link to broader topics over time
- **Privacy-conscious note-taking** — Keeping notes as local files instead of a vendor's cloud database
- **Researchers and writers** — Organizing ideas through backlinks rather than folders

## Who It's For

Logseq is for people who want Notion-style networked note-taking without giving up ownership of their data. It suits privacy-conscious users, researchers, and anyone who prefers plain-text files they can read, back up, or migrate away from at any time, though teams needing Notion's structured databases and collaboration polish may find it a bigger adjustment.
