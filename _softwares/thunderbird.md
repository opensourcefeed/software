## Overview

Thunderbird was first released by the Mozilla Foundation in 2004 as a standalone email client spun out of the Mozilla Application Suite, following the same project split that produced the Firefox browser. Mozilla scaled back direct funding for Thunderbird in 2012 to focus resources on Firefox, transferring stewardship to the Mozilla Foundation and later to MZLA Technologies Corporation, a Mozilla subsidiary created in 2020 specifically to manage Thunderbird's development and any associated revenue.

Since MZLA took over, Thunderbird has seen renewed investment, including a significant interface overhaul ("Supernova," released in 2023) and expansion to mobile platforms with a dedicated Android app (Thunderbird for Android, based on the K-9 Mail codebase Mozilla acquired in 2022). Thunderbird is funded primarily through user donations rather than a commercial license.

## Key Features

- **Standards-based protocols** — IMAP, POP3, SMTP, CalDAV, and CardDAV support for connecting to any compliant mail and calendar server
- **Built-in calendar** — Integrated calendaring via the Lightning component, merged into core Thunderbird
- **Add-ons ecosystem** — Extension system for adding features like enhanced filtering, themes, and encryption tools
- **End-to-end encryption** — Native OpenPGP support built directly into the client
- **Multiple account management** — Unified interface for managing several email accounts from different providers
- **Import tools** — Built-in profile migration from Outlook and other common clients

## Use Cases

- **Personal and small business email** — Managing one or more email accounts across IMAP/POP3 providers
- **Encrypted communication** — Using built-in OpenPGP support for signed and encrypted email
- **Calendar and contact management** — CalDAV/CardDAV sync with self-hosted or third-party calendar servers
- **Migrating away from Outlook** — Standards-based protocol support eases moving mailboxes off Exchange-dependent clients

## Who It's For

Thunderbird is suited to individuals, privacy-conscious users, and small organizations who want a full-featured, no-cost email client without vendor lock-in to a specific mail provider. Its standards-based protocol support makes it a practical alternative to Outlook for anyone not tied to Exchange-specific features, though large enterprises with deep Microsoft 365/Exchange integration needs may still prefer Outlook's native connectivity.
