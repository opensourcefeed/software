## Overview

Nuclear is an open source desktop music player built with Electron, distinct from most items on this list in that it doesn't stream your own files or a licensed catalog — it searches and plays tracks pulled live from free sources such as YouTube and SoundCloud. That makes setup effectively instant: there's no library to import or server to host, just search and play. The trade-off is that catalog availability and audio quality depend on what those source platforms have indexed, and results can occasionally be inconsistent compared to a curated, licensed streaming service.

The project is released under the MIT license and is maintained by a community of contributors on GitHub, with no company or paid tier behind it.

## Key Features

- **No account or subscription** — Search and play immediately with no sign-up
- **Aggregated free sources** — Pulls tracks from YouTube, SoundCloud, and other free platforms
- **Playlists and favorites** — Build and save playlists locally
- **Lyrics and metadata** — Fetches lyrics and track info alongside playback
- **Cross-platform desktop app** — Electron-based builds for Linux, Windows, and macOS
- **Plugin support** — Extend functionality through community plugins

## Use Cases

- **Casual listening without a subscription** — Play music without signing up for a streaming service
- **Discovering tracks across free platforms** — One player instead of switching between YouTube and SoundCloud tabs
- **Lightweight desktop use** — No server setup required, unlike self-hosted options

## Who It's For

Nuclear suits listeners who want a no-cost, no-account way to play music casually without maintaining their own file library or running a self-hosted server. It's a weaker fit for anyone who wants guaranteed catalog completeness or the reliability of a licensed streaming service — for that, or for streaming files you already own, a self-hosted option like Navidrome or Jellyfin is a more predictable choice.
