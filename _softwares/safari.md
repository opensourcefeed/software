## Overview

Safari was introduced by Apple in 2003 as the default browser for Mac OS X, built on WebKit, an engine Apple forked from the open source KHTML project used in KDE's Konqueror browser. WebKit itself was later open-sourced by Apple and went on to become the ancestor of Google's Blink engine (forked from WebKit in 2013), meaning Safari, Chrome, and Chromium-based browsers all trace back to a shared technical lineage even though they've since diverged significantly.

Safari's application layer — the browser UI, its account and sync features (iCloud Keychain, Handoff), and privacy tooling like Intelligent Tracking Prevention — is closed source, distinct from the open source WebKit engine underneath. Apple discontinued Safari for Windows in 2012, and today it's exclusive to macOS and iOS, where it ships as the mandatory default rendering engine for all third-party browsers on iOS due to Apple's App Store policies (a requirement the EU's Digital Markets Act has since forced Apple to loosen within the EU). Safari's tight power-efficiency tuning for Apple hardware and deep iCloud integration are its main draws, at the cost of being unavailable on Windows, Linux, or Android.

## Key Features

- **WebKit engine** — Apple's own tuning of WebKit, optimized for battery efficiency on Mac and iOS hardware
- **Intelligent Tracking Prevention** — Built-in cross-site tracking protection enabled by default
- **iCloud Keychain sync** — Passwords, passkeys, and browsing data synced across Apple devices
- **Handoff** — Continue browsing a tab seamlessly between Mac, iPhone, and iPad
- **Privacy Report** — Built-in dashboard showing trackers blocked per site
- **Extensions via Safari Web Extensions** — Smaller extension catalog than Chromium-based browsers, distributed through the Mac App Store

## Use Cases

- **Apple ecosystem users** — Seamless tab and browsing continuity across Mac, iPhone, and iPad
- **Battery-conscious mobile browsing** — Power efficiency tuned specifically for Apple hardware
- **Privacy-conscious defaults** — Tracking prevention enabled out of the box without configuration
- **iOS browsing** — The underlying engine for all iOS browsers due to Apple's platform requirements (loosened within the EU)

## Who It's For

Safari is aimed at Mac and iOS users who want a browser tightly integrated with Apple's ecosystem and tuned for battery life on Apple hardware. It's distributed free of charge and pre-installed on macOS and iOS, with no versions available for Windows, Linux, or Android.
