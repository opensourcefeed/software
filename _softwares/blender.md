## Overview

Blender was originally developed in the Netherlands by Ton Roosendaal at the animation studio NeoGeo, and was first released publicly in 1994. After the company behind it, Not a Number Technologies, went bankrupt in 2002, Roosendaal founded the non-profit Blender Foundation and ran a "Free Blender" crowdfunding campaign that raised enough money to buy the rights to the source code and release it under the GNU General Public License in October 2002.

Since then, Blender has grown from a niche modeling tool into a full production suite used across the film, game, and visualization industries, with major version 2.8 in 2019 introducing a redesigned interface (Eevee real-time renderer, workspace-based UI) that significantly broadened its adoption. Development is coordinated by the Blender Foundation and its associated Blender Institute, funded through the Blender Development Fund, Blender Studio subscriptions, and corporate members of the Blender Foundation's development fund.

## Key Features

- **Modeling and sculpting** — Polygon modeling, multiresolution sculpting, and a fast dynamic topology sculpt mode
- **Rigging and animation** — Armature-based rigging, non-linear animation editor, and a full keyframe animation system
- **Simulation** — Built-in physics for cloth, fluids, smoke, fire, and rigid/soft body dynamics
- **Rendering** — Two integrated render engines: Eevee (real-time) and Cycles (physically based path tracer, with GPU acceleration)
- **Grease Pencil** — Native 2D drawing and animation tools inside the 3D viewport
- **Video Sequence Editor** — Basic non-linear video editing and compositing built into the same application
- **Python scripting and add-ons** — Full Python API for automation and a large ecosystem of community add-ons

## Use Cases

- **Independent and studio animation** — Short films, series, and VFX shots, including Blender Foundation's own "Open Movie" productions
- **Game asset creation** — Modeling, texturing, and rigging characters and environments for game engines like Godot and Unity
- **Product visualization** — Architectural and product renders using Cycles
- **Motion graphics and VFX** — Compositing and simulation work for video production
- **Education and hobbyist 3D art** — A no-cost entry point into professional-grade 3D tools

## Who It's For

Blender is suited to independent artists, small studios, hobbyists, and educators who want a complete, no-cost 3D production pipeline without per-seat licensing. Its scripting API and active add-on ecosystem also make it attractive to technical artists building custom pipelines, and its growing industry adoption means the skills transfer to studio environments that use it directly.
