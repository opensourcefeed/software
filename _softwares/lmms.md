## Overview

LMMS (Linux MultiMedia Studio) began in 2004, inspired by proprietary loop-based production software like FL Studio, with the goal of bringing a similar beat-making and pattern-sequencing workflow to a free, cross-platform tool. It has since expanded well beyond Linux, with actively maintained Windows and macOS builds alongside its original platform.

Unlike waveform editors or multitrack recording DAWs, LMMS is built primarily around pattern-based composition — arranging beats, melodies, and automation in a piano-roll and song editor, backed by a collection of built-in synthesizers, samplers, and effects rather than focused on recording live audio.

## Key Features

- **Beat/bassline editor** — Pattern-based sequencing for drums and basslines, similar to FL Studio's workflow
- **Built-in synthesizers and samplers** — Includes several native instrument plugins alongside sample playback
- **Piano roll editor** — Full MIDI note editing with support for chords, scales, and automation
- **Plugin support** — Compatible with LADSPA and VST instrument/effect plugins
- **Song editor** — Timeline-based arrangement of patterns, samples, and automation tracks

## Use Cases

- **Beat-making and loop-based production** — Building drum patterns and basslines in a pattern sequencer
- **Electronic music composition** — Synth-driven composition using built-in instruments
- **Learning music production** — A free, low-barrier entry point for beginners exploring DAW workflows

## Who It's For

LMMS suits musicians and hobbyists who want a free, loop- and pattern-based production workflow similar to FL Studio, rather than a live-recording-focused DAW. It's a common starting point for people learning electronic music production without committing to paid software.
