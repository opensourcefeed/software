## Overview

LibreOffice is a free and open source office suite that originated as a fork of OpenOffice.org. Following Oracle's acquisition of Sun Microsystems in early 2010, members of the OpenOffice.org community announced the formation of The Document Foundation on 28 September 2010, an independent organization created to continue development under a more open, community-driven governance model rather than one controlled by a single company. LibreOffice launched publicly in January 2011 and has since become the default office suite for most major Linux distributions.

The suite is dual-licensed under MPLv2 and LGPLv3-or-later, with MPL-2.0 used as the primary SPDX license identifier for the project. LibreOffice ships on two release tracks: a "Fresh" line with newer features and a "Still" line focused on stability, both updated regularly. Development is coordinated by The Document Foundation, a non-profit based in Berlin, and draws on contributions from a large volunteer community as well as corporate backers such as Collabora and Red Hat. LibreOffice reads and writes Microsoft Office formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) alongside the native OpenDocument Format (ODF), and is also available as a mobile viewer app through third-party ports.

## Key Features

- **Writer** — Word processor for documents ranging from letters to full-length books
- **Calc** — Spreadsheet application for data analysis, formulas, and charts
- **Impress** — Presentation software for slideshows with multimedia support
- **Draw** — Vector graphics and diagramming tool
- **Base** — Database management with tables, forms, queries, and reports
- **Math** — Formula editor for typesetting mathematical and scientific notation
- **Format compatibility** — Reads and writes Microsoft Office formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) alongside native OpenDocument Format (ODF)
- **Extensibility** — Supports extensions and macros in Basic, Python, and other scripting languages

## Use Cases

- **Students and educators** — Writing papers, building presentations, and managing spreadsheets without licensing costs
- **Small businesses** — Producing invoices, reports, and correspondence in an office suite compatible with Microsoft Office files
- **Government and public institutions** — Deployments favoring open standards (ODF) and vendor independence
- **Linux users** — Default office suite bundled with most major Linux distributions

## Who It's For

LibreOffice suits individuals, schools, businesses, and government bodies that want a full-featured office suite without subscription fees or vendor lock-in. It is a common choice for organizations prioritizing open document standards and cross-platform compatibility.
