## Overview

ZBrush was created by Pixologic and first released in 1999, introducing a sculpting-focused workflow built around what the company calls "pixols" — pixels that store depth, lighting, and material information alongside color, allowing the software to render detailed 3D-like sculpts efficiently without a traditional GPU-driven 3D pipeline in its early versions. This approach let ZBrush handle sculpts with tens of millions of polygons at a time when consumer hardware struggled with that much geometry in other 3D applications.

ZBrush became the standard tool for high-detail organic sculpting in film creature design, character concept art, and collectibles/miniature production. In 2021, Maxon (the company behind Cinema 4D) acquired Pixologic and has continued developing ZBrush as part of its product lineup, including bringing it to the Apple Silicon Mac platform.

## Key Features

- **Dynamic sculpting** — Handles very high polygon counts through its pixol-based rendering and DynaMesh remeshing
- **Multi-resolution sculpting** — Sculpt at multiple subdivision levels and move detail between them
- **ZRemesher** — Automatic retopology tool for generating clean, animation-ready topology from a sculpt
- **Polypaint** — Direct vertex color painting on the sculpted mesh
- **ZBrush to other DCC pipelines** — GoZ bridge for round-tripping models with Maya, 3ds Max, Blender, and other tools

## Use Cases

- **Character and creature sculpting** — High-detail organic models for film and game production
- **Concept sculpting** — 3D concept art used as a modern alternative to 2D concept sketches
- **Collectibles and miniatures** — Source models for 3D-printed figures and tabletop miniatures
- **Game asset detailing** — Sculpting high-poly assets later baked down to normal maps for real-time use

## Who It's For

ZBrush is aimed at character artists, creature designers, and sculptors working in film, games, and collectibles production who need to work with extremely high polygon counts. Its sculpting-first workflow and perpetual or subscription licensing make it a specialist tool typically used alongside a general-purpose 3D application rather than in place of one.
