## Overview

OpenShot was created by developer Jonathan Thomas, with the first public release in 2008, and has been under continuous open source development since. It's built on top of libopenshot, its own C++ video editing engine (separately MIT-licensed), with a Qt-based interface layered on top under GPL-3.0. The project is aimed squarely at approachability: a drag-and-drop timeline, simple title and transition tools, and a more limited learning curve than full professional suites.

That focus on simplicity is also its main trade-off — OpenShot doesn't attempt to match DaVinci Resolve's color grading depth or Kdenlive's broader effects and compositing options, making it better suited to straightforward cuts, titles, and transitions than complex professional post-production work.

## Key Features

- **Drag-and-drop timeline** — Simple, visual editing aimed at newcomers
- **Titles and transitions** — Built-in animated title templates and transition effects
- **Keyframe animation** — Animate clip properties like position, scale, and rotation over time
- **3D animated titles** — Blender-powered 3D text and title effects
- **Cross-platform** — Native builds for Linux, Windows, and macOS
- **Unlimited tracks** — No hard limit on the number of video/audio tracks in a project

## Use Cases

- **Beginner video editing** — Learning basic non-linear editing without a steep learning curve
- **Simple cuts and titles** — Quick edits, transitions, and text overlays for social or personal video
- **Educational settings** — Teaching editing fundamentals with an approachable free tool

## Who It's For

OpenShot is for people who want a straightforward, free video editor without the depth (or learning curve) of DaVinci Resolve or Kdenlive. It fits beginners, casual creators, and simple editing tasks well; for color grading, advanced compositing, or professional post-production, Kdenlive, Shotcut, or DaVinci Resolve's free tier are better matches.
