## Overview

Fusion 360 launched in 2013 as Autodesk's cloud-connected answer to the maker and product-design market, distinct from the company's older, desktop-only AutoCAD line. Rather than a single discipline like drafting or mechanical modeling, Fusion 360 bundles parametric 3D CAD modeling, CAM (computer-aided manufacturing) toolpaths for CNC machining, and basic simulation and generative design tools into one application, aimed at the individual makers, hobbyists, and small product-design teams who found tools like SolidWorks or Autodesk Inventor too expensive or heavyweight for their needs.

A defining feature of Fusion 360 is its cloud-based data model: projects are stored in Autodesk's cloud by default (with local caching), enabling version history and collaboration features, though this also means an Autodesk account and internet connectivity are required for normal use. Autodesk has offered various free tiers over the years for personal, non-commercial, and startup use, though the terms and inclusions have changed multiple times, drawing frequent criticism from hobbyists who've had previously free access curtailed or moved to paid plans. Commercial use requires a paid subscription license.

## Key Features

- **Parametric 3D modeling** — Feature-based, history-driven CAD modeling similar to SolidWorks and Inventor
- **Integrated CAM** — Generate CNC toolpaths directly from the same model used for design
- **Generative design** — AI-assisted generation of optimized part geometry based on constraints and loads
- **Simulation** — Basic structural and stress simulation tools built into the core application
- **Cloud collaboration** — Version history and multi-user access through Autodesk's cloud data model
- **Sheet metal and rendering tools** — Dedicated workflows for sheet metal design and photorealistic rendering

## Use Cases

- **Product design** — Parametric modeling for consumer products and mechanical parts
- **CNC machining and 3D printing** — Combined design-to-manufacture workflow for makers and small shops
- **Mechanical engineering** — Simulation-assisted part design for functional prototypes
- **Startups and small teams** — Lower-cost entry point into full CAD/CAM compared to Autodesk's enterprise tools

## Who It's For

Fusion 360 is aimed at makers, hobbyists, and small product-design or manufacturing teams who want CAD, CAM, and basic simulation in one cloud-connected application. It requires an Autodesk account and internet connectivity for normal use, with commercial use requiring a paid subscription and free/personal-use tiers subject to change by Autodesk.
