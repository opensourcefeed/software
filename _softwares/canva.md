## Overview

Canva was founded in Sydney, Australia, in 2013 by Melanie Perkins, Cliff Obrecht, and Cameron Adams, growing out of an earlier online yearbook-design tool the founders had built. Its core idea was to replace professional design software's steep learning curve with a browser-based, drag-and-drop editor built around a large library of pre-made templates, aimed at people who need to produce social media graphics, presentations, and marketing materials without formal design training.

Canva is closed source and entirely cloud-hosted; there's no self-hosted or offline version, and the design files, template library, and stock asset catalog all live on Canva's own infrastructure. It's distributed under a freemium model: the free tier covers core editing and a limited template and asset selection, while the paid Canva Pro tier unlocks the full template and stock library, brand kit tools, and AI-assisted features like background removal and image generation.

## Key Features

- **Template library** — Thousands of pre-made layouts for social posts, presentations, documents, and print materials
- **Drag-and-drop editor** — Browser-based design surface aimed at non-designers, with no software to install
- **Stock assets** — Built-in photos, icons, and illustrations, with a larger catalog on paid plans
- **Brand kits** — Save fonts, colors, and logos for consistent use across designs (paid feature)
- **Collaboration** — Real-time multi-user editing and commenting on shared designs
- **AI tools** — Background removal, image generation, and text-to-design features, mostly gated behind Canva Pro

## Use Cases

- **Social media graphics** — Quick production of on-brand posts and stories using templates
- **Presentations and documents** — Slide decks and one-page documents built from layout templates rather than a blank canvas
- **Small business marketing materials** — Flyers, print materials, and brand assets without hiring a designer
- **Non-designers producing visual content** — Anyone who needs a finished design quickly rather than fine-grained manual control

## Who It's For

Canva is aimed at people who want a finished design quickly using templates rather than building one from scratch, including small business owners, marketers, students, and social media managers. It's a weaker fit for anyone who wants to work offline, self-host their design tools, or needs the fine-grained manual control of a dedicated illustration or vector editor, since Canva is built around its cloud template library rather than freeform creation.
