## Overview

darktable was started in 2009 by Johannes Hanika, a graphics researcher, as a personal project for managing and processing his own RAW photo library, deliberately modeled on Lightroom's non-destructive workflow at a time when few open source alternatives existed. The project was released under the GPL-3.0-or-later license and has since grown into a community-maintained application with regular releases, wide camera RAW format support via its own processing pipeline, and packaging across Linux distributions, Windows, and macOS.

Like Lightroom, darktable never modifies the original RAW file; every adjustment is stored as a non-destructive "history stack" that can be edited, reordered, or reset at any time, and multiple processing modules (exposure, tone curve, denoising, lens correction, and dozens more) can be combined and blended per image. The application also includes a library/lighttable view for culling, tagging, and rating large shoots, closely mirroring Lightroom's catalog-and-develop workflow, though its interface and module-based editing model have a steeper learning curve for users coming directly from Lightroom's more streamlined panels.

## Key Features

- **Non-destructive RAW processing** — All edits stored as a reversible history stack; the source RAW file is never altered
- **Extensive RAW support** — Broad camera and lens compatibility through its own demosaicing and color pipeline
- **Modular editing** — Dozens of combinable processing modules for exposure, color, sharpening, and noise reduction
- **Library management** — Culling, star ratings, tagging, and collections for organizing large photo libraries
- **Tethered shooting** — Live capture support for tethered camera shooting sessions
- **Color management** — Full ICC profile support for accurate, print-ready color

## Use Cases

- **RAW photo editing** — Non-destructive development of RAW files from a wide range of camera systems
- **Photo library management** — Culling and organizing large shoots without a subscription
- **Privacy-conscious photographers** — Editing and cataloging locally with no cloud account or telemetry requirement
- **Print and archival workflows** — Full color-managed export pipeline for print production

## Who It's For

darktable is suited to photographers who want Lightroom's non-destructive RAW workflow without a subscription, particularly those comfortable with a more technical, module-based interface. It appeals to Linux users especially, since Lightroom has no native Linux client, as well as privacy-conscious photographers who prefer to keep their library entirely local.
