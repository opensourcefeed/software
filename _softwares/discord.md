## Overview

Discord launched in 2015, founded by Jason Citron and Stan Vishnevskiy, originally targeting gamers who wanted a lower-latency alternative to Skype and TeamSpeak for voice chat during play. Its model of persistent "servers" organized into topic-based text and voice channels, combined with free always-on voice, helped it grow well beyond gaming into communities for open source projects, creators, students, and hobbyist groups of all kinds.

Discord is closed source and runs entirely on the company's own infrastructure — there is no self-hosted option, and all servers, messages, and voice traffic depend on Discord's servers being available. The platform is free to use, monetized through optional Nitro subscriptions for perks like larger file uploads and custom emoji, and more recently through server-level monetization features for community owners.

## Key Features

- **Servers and channels** — Communities organized into text, voice, and forum channels
- **Persistent voice channels** — Drop-in voice chat without needing to schedule a call
- **Screen sharing and video** — Built into both direct messages and voice channels
- **Roles and permissions** — Granular moderation and access control per server
- **Bots and integrations** — A large third-party bot ecosystem for moderation, music, and automation
- **Threads and forums** — Structured discussion within larger communities

## Use Cases

- **Gaming communities** — Voice chat during play alongside text channels
- **Open source and tech communities** — Support and discussion servers as an alternative to mailing lists or forums
- **Creator communities** — Fan and subscriber spaces tied to streaming or content channels
- **Study and hobby groups** — Informal, persistent group chat with voice

## Who It's For

Discord is aimed at communities that want persistent group voice chat combined with organized text discussion, from gaming guilds to open source projects and creator fan communities. It requires a Discord account and depends entirely on the company's hosted servers, which is the main consideration for communities wanting more control over their infrastructure.
