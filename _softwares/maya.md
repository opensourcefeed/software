## Overview

Maya originated at Alias Systems Corporation, which released the first version in 1998 after merging technology from several earlier animation packages, including Alias's own PowerAnimator and technology acquired from Wavefront Technologies. Autodesk acquired Alias in 2006 and has developed Maya since as part of its media and entertainment product line. It has become one of the standard tools used in film visual effects, animated feature production, and AAA game development.

Maya is distributed under a subscription licensing model, with pricing tiers for individuals, small teams, and enterprise studios. It is commonly used alongside other Autodesk products such as Arnold (its default renderer) and 3ds Max, and integrates with major game engines like Unity and Unreal Engine through plugin pipelines.

## Key Features

- **Polygon and NURBS modeling** — Tools for building detailed hard-surface and organic 3D geometry
- **Rigging and skinning** — Skeleton, joint, and deformer systems for character animation setup
- **Animation tools** — Keyframe animation, graph editor, and non-linear animation layers
- **Simulation** — Built-in dynamics for cloth, fluids, hair, and particle effects (nCloth, nParticles)
- **Arnold renderer** — Integrated physically based renderer for production-quality output
- **Scripting and pipeline integration** — Python and MEL scripting APIs for custom tools and studio pipelines

## Use Cases

- **Film and TV VFX studios** — Character animation, creature rigging, and visual effects shots
- **Game studios** — Modeling and animating characters and assets for AAA game production
- **Motion graphics and previsualization** — Building 3D assets and animatics for commercials and previz
- **Education** — Taught in many 3D animation and visual effects degree programs

## Who It's For

Maya is aimed at professional 3D artists, riggers, and animators working in film, television, and game studios where deep pipeline integration and industry-standard tooling are required. Its subscription cost and steep learning curve make it primarily a studio and professional tool rather than a hobbyist option.
