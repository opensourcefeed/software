## Overview

QuickTime Player is the default multimedia player included with macOS. Originally released in 1991 as part of the QuickTime media framework, the standalone player continues to ship with every Mac. It provides clean, minimal playback for video and audio files, along with built-in screen recording, basic video trimming, and export to common formats.

QuickTime for Windows was discontinued by Apple in 2016, leaving the player exclusively on macOS. Its format support is limited compared to cross-platform alternatives — it handles MOV, MP4, M4V, and a handful of audio formats but lacks codec support for MKV, FLAC, and many other common container and codec combinations.

## Key Features

- **Screen recording** — Record the full screen, a selected area, or an iOS device connected via cable
- **Video trimming** — Split and trim clips directly in the player without additional editing software
- **Audio recording** — Record audio through the built-in microphone or external input
- **Export and sharing** — Export to 4K, 1080p, 720p, and audio-only formats; share directly to Mail, Messages, and AirDrop
- **Picture-in-picture** — Float video playback on top of other windows
- **Rotation and flipping** — Rotate or flip video orientation from within the player

## Use Cases

- **Mac users** — Quick, no-setup playback of video and audio files already compatible with macOS codecs
- **Screen recording** — Built-in tool for recording tutorials, presentations, and app demos
- **Quick trimming** — Fast, simple video trimming without launching a full editor like iMovie

## Who It's For

QuickTime Player is for macOS users who need a built-in, no-installation media player for common formats and quick screen recording. It is proprietary, macOS-only, and limited in format support. Users who need broader codec support, cross-platform playback, or open source licensing should consider alternatives like VLC, mpv, or IINA.
