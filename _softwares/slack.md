## Overview

Slack originated as an internal communication tool built by Tiny Speck, a company founded by Stewart Butterfield and colleagues in 2009. Tiny Speck's first product was Glitch, a browser-based multiplayer game that shut down in December 2012 after failing to gain a sustaining audience. The team repurposed the messaging and file-sharing tools it had built for its own use during Glitch's development into a standalone product, launching Slack publicly in August 2013.

Slack Technologies was acquired by Salesforce in a deal announced on December 1, 2020, and completed on July 21, 2021, at a valuation of approximately $27.7 billion — Salesforce's largest acquisition to date. Slack continues to operate as a distinct product under Salesforce, organizing team conversations into channels, supporting direct messaging and threaded discussions, and integrating with hundreds of third-party services including Google Drive, GitHub, Jira, and Zoom.

## Key Features

- **Channels** — Organized conversations by topic, team, or project
- **Direct messaging** — One-on-one and group direct messages
- **Threads** — Organized discussions within channels
- **File sharing** — Upload and share files with preview support
- **Integrations** — Connect with Google Drive, GitHub, Jira, Zoom, and hundreds more
- **Search** — Full-text search across messages and files
- **Workflow Builder** — Automate routine tasks without code
- **Slack Connect** — Collaborate with external organizations

## Use Cases

- **Development teams** — Code reviews, deployment notifications, and team chat
- **Marketing teams** — Campaign coordination and asset sharing
- **Remote teams** — Async communication and team updates
- **Customer support** — Internal coordination and ticket routing

## Who It's For

Slack is designed for teams and organizations of all sizes that need structured communication. It is particularly popular with tech companies, startups, and remote-first organizations. The free tier limits access to message history to the most recent 90 days and caps the number of connected apps and integrations; paid tiers unlock unlimited history and additional administrative and security features.
